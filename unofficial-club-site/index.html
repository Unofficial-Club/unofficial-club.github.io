Why "unofficial"

We're not a registered student org, and that's on purpose. No forms, no faculty sign-off, no committee or nobody deciding what has worth. Just a room, a whiteboard, and whoever has something to say.

The Simple Idea

We're just a bunch of students who got tired of sitting on the stuff we find interesting. Everyone here is pushing ahead in some different direction — deep into their own thing — and picking up ideas the rest of us never get a chance to see. We wanted a way to actually cross paths, so people who are into the same thing can find each other, and people who want to jump into a field someone else is already deep in have a way in. Every field is moving faster than any one place can keep up with, and that's fine — the point isn't catching up. It's sharing what we've already found, and pulling apart the ideas we're not sure are right yet.

So someone shows up and explains how the Burj Khalifa stays standing, or how your own immune system turns on you, or why a rotating detonation engine works the way it does. Someone else walks through the Lagrangian they wish they'd seen sooner — and then straight into using it in a robotics simulation. Someone thinks quantum mechanics is missing something and wants to argue their case on a whiteboard.

It's incredibly informal. No slides, no script. Show up one day, and start explaining. And you don't need anything prepared to show up — most people are just there to listen and see where it goes.

How it actually works

The format

Show up, take 5 to 15 minutes, talk about whatever you're into — preferably STEM, not strictly required. No slides needed, nothing to submit.

The connections

Give a talk on something you love, and you'll meet the other people in the room who've been quietly working on something related, or have been wanting to start.

The archive

Every session gets recorded. Couldn't make it, or want to catch a talk from before you joined? It's sitting in the records, drop-in only.

The notes

Some talks leave more behind than a recording. Full write-ups, projects, slides, books, papers — if it's worth passing along, it goes here too.
